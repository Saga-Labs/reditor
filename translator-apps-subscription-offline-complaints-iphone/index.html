Translate Now alternatives without a surprise subscription (2026)

15 November 2026 · Complaints from 157 recent App Store reviews of four translator apps, and a check to do before you pay or travel.

To choose a translator without a surprise subscription, read the trial and renewal terms on its App Store page before you download, set a reminder for the day the trial ends, and know where to cancel: Settings, your name, Subscriptions. Test offline mode at home before a trip, because several apps fail there.

In 157 one- and two-star reviews of Translate Now, Google Translate, Microsoft Translator and iTranslate, the most common complaints were subscriptions and charges (most of all for Translate Now), offline mode that does not work as expected, and network errors. Each has a check below.

What we looked at

We read the most recent one- and two-star reviews of four translation apps on the US App Store: Translate Now - AI Translator (30 reviews), Google Translate (55), Microsoft Translator (57) and iTranslate Translator (15). That is 157 reviews in all, the most recent reviews of each app (up to 100 per app), downloaded as of Nov 15, 2026. Three groups come up again and again: money (subscriptions, paywalls, charges after cancelling), offline mode that does not work as expected, and network errors.

  • Subscriptions, paywalls and charges: about half of the Translate Now reviews (14 of 30), and 4 of 15 for iTranslate.
  • Offline or “no internet” problems: 5 of 55 for Google Translate and 6 of 57 for Microsoft Translator.
  • Wrong or poor translations: 10 of 55 for Google Translate.

Reviews are the opinions of people who were unhappy. They are not a test of the apps.

“Charged after I cancelled” and the trial (the biggest group)

This is the biggest group for Translate Now. A reviewer: “I canceled the subscription and it still charged me” (Translate Now, 1 star). Another: “Did not realize they were taking $10.98 a WEEK on a subscription fee” (Translate Now, 1 star). For iTranslate, the complaint is the trial: “Be sure to cancel after trial or pay $40 per month every month!” (iTranslate, 1 star). Some reviewers say the free part is too small: “You have to pay for common languages” (Translate Now, 1 star). These are the reviewers' words, not facts we checked in each app.

The App Store pages say what each app sells. Translate Now's page promotes a subscription called Air Apps One. iTranslate's page says a free trial turns into a paid subscription unless auto-renew is turned off at least 24 hours before the end of the trial.

Fix, before you download and before any trial:

  1. Scroll to the end of the App Store page and read the subscription terms: the price, how often it renews and when a free trial ends.
  2. Set a calendar reminder for the day before the trial ends.
  3. To check or stop a subscription: open Settings, tap your name, tap Subscriptions, tap the subscription, then Cancel Subscription. Apple's steps are on Cancel a subscription from Apple. Deleting the app does not cancel it.
  4. If you were charged after cancelling, you can ask Apple for a refund: Request a refund for apps or content that you bought from Apple. Apple decides.

“Doesn't work offline” (Google Translate, Microsoft Translator)

A translator is most useful when you have no signal, on a plane or in a foreign subway. The reviews say that is when several apps fail. A Google Translate reviewer: “It DOES NOT work offline as advertised…” (1 star). Another, in Japan: “This app was unusable if /when I wasn't connected to the internet” (2 stars). A Microsoft Translator reviewer says the app “removed the ability to download languages for offline use” (1 star).

iTranslate's App Store page says the same thing from its side: to use offline mode, you need to download language packs first.

Fix: test offline mode at home, on Wi-Fi, before you travel. Turn Wi-Fi and mobile data off, open the app, and translate one sentence. If it fails, the language pack is missing or the app does not offer offline mode for that language. Check the app's own settings for downloadable languages, and check the App Store page first if you cannot find them.

“Network error” (Microsoft Translator)

Microsoft Translator has the most network complaints in this set, mostly about its live conversation mode: “Keeps getting network error when trying to use live translation” (2 stars), from a reviewer who was on Wi-Fi. Another reviewer says the app kept asking them to check the network while they were on Wi-Fi.

Fix: close the app fully, turn Wi-Fi off and on, and try with mobile data. If it still fails, use the text mode, which is slower but often works. If you need a reliable offline mode, decide before you travel, not in the middle of a conversation.

Before you download or travel: a 5-line check

  • Did I read the subscription terms and the trial length on the App Store page before I downloaded?
  • Did I set a reminder for the day before the trial ends?
  • Did I test offline mode at home, with Wi-Fi and mobile data off?
  • Did I check which languages I can download and whether I need them before I leave?
  • Do I know where to cancel (Apple: cancel a subscription) and how to ask for a refund (Apple: request a refund)?

Frequently asked questions

Which translator app has the fewest subscription complaints?

The reviews do not name a winner. In this set, Translate Now had the most subscription and charge complaints (14 of 30 recent one- and two-star reviews). Read each app's App Store subscription terms before you download.

Does Google Translate work offline on iPhone?

Some reviewers say it does not work as advertised offline. Test it at home with Wi-Fi and mobile data turned off, before a trip, and check which languages you can download.

How do I cancel a translator app subscription?

On iPhone, open Settings, tap your name, tap Subscriptions, tap the subscription and tap Cancel Subscription. Apple explains it on its page about cancelling a subscription.

Can I get a refund for a charge after I cancelled?

You can ask Apple. Its page on requesting a refund for apps or content bought from Apple explains how; the decision is Apple's.
